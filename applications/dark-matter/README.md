Dark Matter from Holographic Saturation
Application of the ORT framework to the dark matter problem.

Key Idea
When comoving regions in the early Universe approach the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy bound during the radiation epoch, a fraction of degrees of freedom decouple from Standard Model gauge interactions while remaining gravitationally coupled through the stress–energy tensor.

In this framework, dark matter is interpreted as an archival sector of degrees of freedom (“cosmic tailings”), not as new particle species or a modification of gravity.

Current Findings (conceptual)
Primordial origin
In the current framework the main dark matter component is generated at very early times (T_dec ~ 10^16 GeV, before BBN and recombination).

Constraint on BSM
Matching Ω_DM/Ω_b ≈ 5.4 requires a total effective degree-of-freedom count g_total(T_sat) ~ 5×10^2 and a decoupled fraction of order ~80%. This is natural for GUT / string scenarios and excludes minimal SUSY alone as the sole source of the dark sector.

CDM-like behavior
The archival sector has an effective equation of state w ≈ 0 (argued on physical grounds), clusters like standard CDM, and in principle can reproduce NFW-like halo profiles.

No significant late-time DM production
Late-time entropy production (stars, black holes) adds only a small correction to the primordial dark matter mass budget (≲ few % in current estimates). Most of the dark sector is primordial, not generated by galactic astrophysics.

All these points are hypotheses and require testing with detailed simulations and comparison to data.
